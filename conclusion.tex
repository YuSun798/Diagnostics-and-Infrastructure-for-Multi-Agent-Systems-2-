Foundation model-based multi-agent systems increasingly depend on two operational layers that have largely developed apart: diagnostics, which explains and constrains failures, and infrastructure, which keeps execution efficient. This review brings them into one runtime-stack view, organizing diagnostics into failure localization, observability, safety, and benchmarks, and infrastructure into memory optimization, prefix-aware scheduling, cache lifecycle, and power management. Quantitative results are reported with explicit verification depth, and heterogeneous benchmarks are treated as a landscape rather than a global leaderboard.

The main synthesis is that the two layers act on overlapping runtime objects under different objectives. Diagnostics benefits from preserving traces, context, and replayable state; serving infrastructure benefits from reclaiming or compressing state under resource budgets. The surveyed evidence makes the retention tension concrete through two cross-layer mechanisms---eviction and snapshot retention---with trace compression providing an intra-layer analogue and TraceElephant quantifying the sensitivity of attribution to observability. No surveyed system manages diagnostic value and serving value through a shared retention budget. Treating retention as an explicit cross-layer resource is therefore a concrete research direction rather than a settled result. The taxonomy, evidence coding, validation protocol, and four co-design directions developed here provide a common frame for testing that idea in future systems.
